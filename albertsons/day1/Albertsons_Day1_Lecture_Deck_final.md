<overview>
This is Dr. Anand Jayaraman's Day 1 lecture deck for the Albertsons 3.5-Day Enterprise AI Architecture Intensive, delivered by InsightVerge/Protiviti to a cohort of Albertsons enterprise/solution architects. It is a 1.5-hour, framework-first lecture (roughly 31 slides) that runs before the afternoon hands-on labs, deliberately built to avoid repeating the specific retail case (a Store 184-style scenario) the hands-on session uses, so the two sessions reinforce rather than duplicate each other.

The deck is organized into three segments, named explicitly on the roadmap slide (slide 2):

1. Hype, Reality, and the AI Crush (slides 3-7): opens with a fictional grocery CEO asking ChatGPT "how much should I discount?" and getting a confident, ungrounded answer. Establishes that frontier AI capability is genuinely extraordinary (real screenshots of Anthropic, Quanta Magazine, and Axios posts), that this capability mostly fails to show up as enterprise value (MIT NANDA's 95%-zero-P&L-impact stat), and introduces the first of four "value killers": the AI Crush, falling for a demo rather than a pain point.

2. Three Types of AI (slides 8-20, "the heart of today," about 40 minutes): builds up Rules/deterministic logic, supervised ML, unsupervised ML, deep learning, price intelligence sensing, reinforcement learning, and GenAI from first principles using one continuous retail narrative (a reorder-point rule, then the CEO's discount question re-answered properly via ML simulation, then a pricing RL loop, then a supplier-compliance GenAI example), culminating in a "hybrid by design" slide and a technology triage ladder (rules → ML → GenAI, climb only when the lower rung fails).

3. Why Architects Should Care (slides 21-31): returns to the CEO's discount question as a Strategy → Decision → Workflow → Technology → Owner chain, walks the three remaining value killers (Roadless Ferrari / no integration, Empty Pantry / weak production data, Spider-Man Standoff / no named owner), recaps all four killers, introduces the Production Chasm (Prototype → Product → Operating Capability, with IBM Watson as the cautionary tale), the Four Gate Questions before any sprint, an expectation-management/benchmarking framework (bicycle/Dacia/Jaguar), the Netflix Prize as a lesson that accuracy is not value, MLOps as "validity over time," and closes by handing the room off to the afternoon's hands-on case.

Two slides (8 "AI today" knowledge-types graphic, and 28 "Expectation Management" bicycle/Dacia/Jaguar benchmarking slide) are visually distinct from the rest of the deck's dark-green/mint InsightVerge theme, likely sourced from outside the main build; flagged here for awareness, not corrected.

Note on internal consistency: slide 27's closing note line promises the next mechanism is "a one-page architecture decision record" (ADR), but the two ADR-specific slides that existed in an earlier version of this deck (an ADR-instrument slide and a worked CEO-discount ADR example) were removed in this final cut. Slide 27 flows straight into slide 28 (benchmarking) instead, so that closing sentence in the notes no longer has a slide to land on if read literally, worth knowing if this deck gets reused or re-narrated later.

Audience: Albertsons enterprise/solution architects, strong on architecture and GenAI/LLM APIs, weaker on hands-on ML/deep learning — the deck is pitched at "what each AI type is and when to reach for it," never at model-building mechanics, consistent with the program design.
</overview>
